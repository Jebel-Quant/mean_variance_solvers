\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Guessing a whole active set at once and repairing it from the signs that come back
--- primal--dual active set, block principal pivoting --- terminates finitely for
the bound-constrained quadratic program, because the system it solves on a
candidate set is then a principal submatrix of $G^{-1}$ and inherits that matrix's
$P$-matrix property. Our first result is that this is exactly what the general
constraint class costs. For $C\T x \ge b$ the working-set system is
$C_\Active\T G^{-1} C_\Active$, positive definite only when $C_\Active$ has full
column rank, which is a property of the \emph{guess} and not of the data; the
$P$-matrix property is lost, and with it the guarantee. The obstruction is
structural rather than a missing anti-cycling rule.

That exposure is easy to mistake for a technicality, and we show it is not. Over
six hundred random instances across four constraint families it never occurs ---
which is why it can be overlooked --- while duplicating columns of $C$, as any
programmatically assembled model may, raises it to $93\%$, at which point the
certified fraction collapses from $100\%$ to nothing.

To make that argument precise we first give a self-contained derivation of the
Goldfarb--Idnani dual method, organised around a single matrix $J$ satisfying
$J\T G J = I$ and $J\T A = [R;0]$. Every quantity the iteration computes follows
from those two lines and admits a representation-free closed form, so any $(J,R)$
meeting the invariants produces the same iterate; the objective increment along a
step of length $t$ is exactly $t(t/2 + u_*)\,z\T G z$; and when the primal cannot
move and no multiplier can be reduced, the vector $r$ the solver already holds is a
Farkas certificate, so the infeasibility verdict is a proof. All \qpIdentCount{}
identities are then checked against a running implementation, on its own internal
state, over \qpIdentChecks{} evaluations.

What makes the unguaranteed methods usable is that strict convexity makes the KKT
conditions sufficient, so every candidate can be certified rather than trusted. On
long-only portfolio data the active set reaches $\qpPfSpActive{}$ of
$\qpPfSpN{}+1$ constraints, against the small sets the synthetic families common
in this literature produce.
\end{abstract}
