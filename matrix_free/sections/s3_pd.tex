\section{Positive-Definite Reduction}\label{sec:pd}

Ignoring the inequality constraints for now, the Lagrangian for the equality-constrained problem is:
\[
\mathcal{L}(w, \lambda) = \frac{\|Xw\|^2}{T} - \rho\hat\mu^\top w - \lambda^\top(Bw - c)\,,
\]
whose stationarity condition is
$2\hat\Sigma\, w = B^\top\lambda + \rho\hat \mu$, where $\hat\Sigma = X^\top X/T$.
Together with feasibility $Bw = c$ this forms an $(n+p)\times(n+p)$ saddle-point system,
but the indefinite system is never needed: the multipliers are eliminated through a
$p\times p$ Schur complement, following the general reduction of the companion
paper~\cite{schmelzer2026nncg}. Stationarity expresses the weights through $p+1$ SPD
solves against $\hat\Sigma$,
\[
  w = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_1\lambda + \tfrac{\rho}{2}\,v_2\,,
  \qquad
  V_1 = \hat\Sigma^{-1}B^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
  \quad(\text{one solve per column}),
  \qquad
  v_2 = \hat\Sigma^{-1}\hat\mu\,,
\]
and imposing $Bw = c$ fixes the multipliers through the $p\times p$ system
\[
  S\,\lambda = 2c - \rho\,Bv_2\,,
  \qquad
  S = BV_1 = B\hat\Sigma^{-1}B^\top \succ 0\,.
\]
The Schur complement $S$ is SPD because $\hat\Sigma \succ 0$ and $B$ has full row rank,
and the $p\times p$ solve is a dense Cholesky, negligible for the small $p$ typical of
balance conditions. For $\rho = 0$ the solve $v_2$ is not needed and the weights collapse
to $w = V_1 S^{-1} c$, a total of $p$ SPD solves.

For the budget constraint ($p = 1$, $B = \mathbf{1}^\top$, $c = 1$) the solve
$V_1 = v_1 = \hat\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf{1}$ is a single vector and the Schur complement is the
scalar $S = \mathbf{1}^\top v_1$. The weights specialise to
\[
  w = \frac{v_1}{\mathbf{1}^\top v_1} \quad (\rho = 0)\,,
  \qquad\qquad
  w = \tfrac{\lambda}{2}\,v_1 + \tfrac{\rho}{2}\,v_2\,,
  \quad
  \lambda = \frac{2 - \rho\,(\mathbf{1}^\top v_2)}{\mathbf{1}^\top v_1} \quad (\rho \neq 0)\,,
\]
the two formulas implemented by every solver in Section~\ref{sec:solvers}.

In all cases the multipliers are recovered analytically rather than as part of the solve.
The long-only problem therefore reduces to solving a symmetric positive definite system
$\hat\Sigma\,v = b$ for $p$ or $p+1$ right-hand sides (one or two in the budget case),
subject to the remaining inequality
constraint $w \geq 0$; the latter is addressed by the active-set loop of
Section~\ref{sec:primaldual}. The right-hand sides share the operator
$\hat\Sigma$, so the solves are warm-started against each other at no extra cost.
Section~\ref{sec:precond} shows how Ledoit-Wolf
shrinkage replaces $\hat\Sigma$ with a better-conditioned estimator $\hat\Sigma_{\mathrm{LW}}$
without changing this reduction.
