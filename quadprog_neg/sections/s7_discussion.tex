\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The result is a small one and it is worth stating its size accurately.
Proposition~\ref{prop:guessrank} is an elementary rank observation with a two-line
proof. That the plain block exchange fails on general convex quadratic programs is
known, and repaired, in~\cite{curtis2015pdas}. What is added here is the location
of the failure and the price of ignoring it: the working-set system is positive
definite exactly when the guessed normals are independent, which is a property of
the guess rather than of the data, so the $P$-matrix structure that carries the
bound-constrained theorem is gone and no reordering brings it back; and the guess
that breaks is the one the method's own starting rule produces on duplicated
constraints, at a rate of $\qpPdasSingularDup\%$ where random instances show
$\qpPdasSingularBox\%$.

Three consequences seem worth drawing.

\emph{The bound-constrained intuition does not transfer, and the reason is
specific.} Block principal pivoting on $x \ge 0$ is safe not because the pivots
are small or the sets well chosen but because $G^{-1}$ is a $P$-matrix, which is a
statement about all $2^n$ candidate sets at once. Reading across from that setting
to $C\T x \ge b$ replaces a statement about the data with a statement about each
guess, and the two are not the same kind of hypothesis.

\emph{A rank test is not optional, and the factorisation already is one.} An
implementation that solves~\eqref{eq:pdassolve} by a route tolerant of rank
deficiency will return a plausible vector on a dependent guess and has converted a
detectable failure into a silent one. Cholesky refuses, which is the behaviour to
want.

\emph{Certification is the right replacement, not a fallback of last resort.}
Strict convexity makes the Karush--Kuhn--Tucker conditions sufficient
(Proposition~\ref{prop:sufficient}), so the exchange can be run as a heuristic
proposer whose output is verified at $O(nm)$ and discarded when it fails. On the
families measured, that arrangement gives the iteration count of the guess and the
guarantees of the walk, and the two are not in tension because they are sequential
rather than alternative. The general form of the observation is that a
method which may fail but can recognise its own success needs a certificate and
not a convergence theory.

Two limits should be kept in view. The scope here is $G \succ 0$; the dual walk
extends to semidefinite $G$ with finite termination by Boland~\cite{boland1997}
and by proximal regularisation in~\cite{arnstrom2022daqp}, and we have not asked
what the rank condition looks like there, where $G^{-1}$ does not exist and the
dual Hessian must be formed differently. And the diagnosis in
Section~\ref{ssec:structural} quantifies over rules for choosing the next set, not
over algorithms: a method that carries state beyond the active-set estimate, which
is precisely what~\cite{curtis2015pdas} does, is outside its scope and is not
contradicted by it.
